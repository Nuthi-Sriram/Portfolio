The Vault CRM to PromoMats integration runs through CrossLinks, which packages custom source files so CLM presentations and rich media transfer with full fidelity. When that stopped working reliably, the symptoms were bad but vague: syncs that used to finish in a reasonable window were taking more than four hours, and a backlog of over 80,000 messages had piled up behind them. Nothing was erroring. There was no stack trace pointing at a cause, no exception log to grep for a culprit. The system was just slow and getting slower, and files with international filenames were coming through corrupted.

That absence of an error was the actual problem to solve. A crash tells you where to look; silence doesn't. I worked backward from the corruption itself rather than from a log line, narrowing down which files broke and what they had in common, until the pattern pointed at the filenames specifically — and past that, at how those filenames were traveling through the request. The root cause turned out to be Tomcat silently dropping non-ASCII HTTP headers. Filenames with international characters were being carried in headers, and when a header contained characters outside plain ASCII, Tomcat discarded it without complaint instead of rejecting or flagging the request. The application never saw a bad value; it saw a missing one, and had no way to distinguish that from a header that was never sent.

Once the drop was identified, the fix followed directly: stop relying on non-ASCII HTTP headers to carry filename data through the request path. With that source of corruption removed, the sync backlog stopped growing on its own — the pipeline had been healthy the whole time, just repeatedly getting handed bad or missing data for a subset of files and stalling on it. Draining the existing backlog and confirming clean syncs closed out the incident: the 80,000-message backlog cleared, and the four-hour-plus sync times went away along with it. The harder part wasn't writing the fix; it was building enough confidence in a silent failure mode to know where to stop looking.
